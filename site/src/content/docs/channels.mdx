Conversations in Huddle are either channels or direct messages. This page explains the difference and how to manage both.

## Public and private channels

| | Public channel | Private channel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shown with | `#` | A lock |
| Who can find it | Everyone on the team except guests | Only its members |
| How you get in | Join it yourself | Someone inside adds you |
| Can you read before joining | Yes | No |

Everyone starts in **#general**, the channel for announcements and everyday chat. It can't be archived.

## Create a channel

1. In the sidebar, select **Add a channel** next to **Channels**.
2. Select **Create a channel**.
3. Type a **Name**, for example *launch-plans*, and an optional **Topic**.
4. Under **Who can find it**, choose **Public** or **Private**.
5. Select **Create channel**.

Channel names use lowercase letters, numbers, dashes and underscores, up to 80 characters. Huddle turns spaces into dashes for you. Guests can't create channels.

## Join a public channel

Select **Add a channel** to open **Browse channels**. Select a channel to read it first, or **Join** to join it straight away. While you're only reading, a bar at the bottom says you can **Join channel** to post and get its notifications.

## Channel details

Select the people count in the top-right corner of a channel to open its details. There you can:

- Change the **Topic**. Anyone in the channel can do this.
- Change the **Name**. Only the person who created the channel and admins can.
- Choose your **Notifications** for this channel (see [Notifications](/docs/notifications)).
- See the members and **Add people**. Guests can't add people, and only owners and admins can add a guest to a channel.
- **Remove** someone (owners and admins only). They stop seeing new messages there.
- **Leave channel**. For a private channel, someone inside has to add you back.
- **Archive channel** (its creator and admins). It disappears from everyone's sidebar and nobody can post, but its history stays searchable.

If a guest is in a conversation, a note at the top says so, so you always know when someone from outside your team can read it.

## Direct messages

Direct messages (DMs) are private conversations between up to nine people.

1. In the sidebar, select **New message** next to **Direct messages**.
2. Pick one person, or up to eight for a group message.
3. Select **Message**.

The same group of people always gets the same conversation, so starting a new message to someone you've written to before opens your existing DM. A DM with just yourself is your own space for notes, drafts and reminders.

You can't leave, rename or archive a DM. Guests can only message people they share a channel with.
